Documentation

Which document answers which question

The repository holds roughly 8,700 lines of design across nine parts, plus a handful of files that record what was actually built and what went wrong. They do not all agree, and the order in which they win is written down.

The specification is the design. The tracker is the state. The spec runs ahead of the build in most places and, in a few, the build went the other way. Never conclude from a spec section that something is implemented.

The supersession rule

Read it bottom-up. Where two documents disagree, the lower one wins:

  1. Parts 1–6 — written in order, later parts superseding earlier ones.
  2. Part 7 — re-scopes the whole thing from a commercial product to solo self-use, and supersedes Part 4 entirely.
  3. Parts 8 and 9 — written after Part 7, and superseding it in their own areas (the model runtime, and what may leave the machine).
  4. The design documents — GUI, UX and LLD win over the spec for the surfaces they cover.
  5. DECISIONS.md — supersedes all of them. Where building it proved a part wrong, the decision is the answer and the spec text was deliberately left alone.

That last one matters more than it sounds. Five entries in DECISIONS reverse things the spec still says: SQLite FTS5 rather than Tantivy, PDFKit rather than PDFium, an MLX sidecar rather than Candle or Ollama, a desktop app that had been refused, and an agent layer that had been refused twice and got built anyway. Annotating rather than rewriting is how supersession is recorded here.

Start with these four

Everything else is design. These are the files that describe reality.

DocumentWhat it is for
ARCHITECTURE.md How it is actually built, in under 200 lines: the fourteen crates and why the seams are where they are, where the data lives, how a file becomes a citable chunk, how a question becomes an answer, and a table of things deliberately not built. Written from the code. Start here.
TRACKER.md The live checklist, and the file that actually gets updated. Current milestone at the top, a log entry whenever something surprised the author, and the adversarial corpus. If you want to know whether a feature exists, this is the answer.

BUGS.md Open findings — what is wrong right now, and specifically what each one misleads you into believing. The distinction between a wrong number and a number that is true about something other than what you assumed is most of this file.

The specification, nine parts

Its own index and reading order live in docs/README.md. Read Part 7 first — Parts 1–6 were written for a commercial multi-user product, and reading them without it will send you building the wrong thing.

PartCovers
7 · Solo re-scope §123–135. What was cut, and the fourteen things that must not be. The MCP-first inversion. §126 is the list of non-negotiables — the one to read if you are touching files, paths, evidence or execution.
1 · Master specification §1–43. Vision, requirements, trust model, architecture, data model, ingestion, retrieval, transactions, ADRs, threat model.
2 · Gap closure §44–60. Cloud placeholders, watcher limits, verification, reversibility, SQLite writes, and an honest posture on sandboxing.
3 · Conversion & multimodal §61–76. Parser tiers and provenance classes (§63), OCR, images, embedded metadata. Video and audio are deferred.
4 · Commercial §77–92. SKUs, pricing, support, compliance. Superseded by Part 7 in full, and parked rather than deleted in case that ever changes.
5 · Capabilities §93–104. Prior art, hardware probe, local LLM, execution tiers, file intelligence, and §99, tables. Amended by Part 7 on execution and hardware.
6 · Engineering reference §105–122. The one you build from: SQLite DDL, migrations, the error taxonomy, config, jobs, chunking, fusion (§113) and the context envelope (§114).
8 · Model runtime §136–150. Hardware probe and live sampling, the model registry, dynamic loading, the supervisor — admission, circuit breaker, queues — and cloud providers.
9 · Egress §151+. What may leave the machine and the fetch tool, with the policy written before the implementation on purpose.

Design documents and post-mortems

Written after the spec. For the surfaces they cover, they win over it.

DocumentWhat it is for
GUI.md The desktop app: stack, the Tauri boundary, information architecture, interaction, visual direction, performance budgets. §4 bounds what ships.
UX.md The terminal and MCP surfaces: command shapes, how results render, error wording, machine-readable output, exit codes.
LLD.md Internals: layering, patterns used and rejected, concurrency, error strategy, seams, testing.
Comparison.md Prior art on the safety axes — provenance, injection, write tools, egress, self-citation. Where the differentiators were argued for, and where "hybrid retrieval" was ruled out as one of them.
RESEARCH_LANDSCAPE.md Prior art on the product axis: what eight chat, RAG and agent products are actually like to use, and §6, a list of features refused with reasons.
Decision_MLX_Runtime.md The long form behind the choice of a bundled MLX sidecar over Candle or Ollama.
UI_AUDIT.md Four independent passes over the desktop app — shell, Ask, configurability, design system — with contrast ratios computed rather than estimated and every claim carrying a file:line.
LESSONS.md Bug shapes that are not specific to this codebase — values nobody reads, checks that cannot fail, tests that look like coverage. Marrow supplied the evidence; the shapes are general.
